An aeroplane manufacturing company needs to determine how many hours per day they should utilize the 3 robots they have available. Each robot has different energy requirements, operating costs, and capabilities that are presented in the following table (all data is given per hour of operation)

The company needs to install at least 1,000,000 rivets and complete 12,800 feet of welding per day. Total energy consumed by the robots each day cannot exceed 500 kWh, and each robot can work up to 20 hours per day because of maintenance requirements.

Jean Farell, the operation manager of the company and a graduate, has tried to use optimization to find the optimal utilization mix of each one of the three robots. She has developed the spreadsheet shown in figure 1.1 and used Solver to find the mix that minimises the total cost (1.2 shows the formula used in the spreadsheet). 1.4 The Solver sensitivity analysis

a) What is the solution that minimises the cost (objective function and optimal values

of the variable).

The optimal mix results in a total cost of $219.5. This is achieved by using robot type 1 for 1.22 hours and robot type 3 for 9.76 hours.

b) What is the meaning of a binding constraint? Which constraints are binding?

A binding constraint is a constraint that is exactly satisfied at the optimal solution, directly limiting the value of the objective function. In this case rivets and energy are binding constraints.

c) What is the meaning of the reduced cost of robot type 2?
The reduced cost indicates how much the objective function coefficient would need to improve before the variable robot type 2 would enter the optimal solution. In other words, if we were to introduce robot type in the optimal mix, the cost would increase of additional $159.02, bringing it to a total of $378.53.

d) Why is the reduced cost of robot type 1 and 3 equal to zero?

The two robots are included in the optimal mix, therefore they will not change the value of the objective by including them in the mix.

e) Evaluate the impact of increasing the limit on energy from 500 to 520. Why is the shadow price of welding equal to zero? The shadow price of the constraint is -1.95. This means that for every extra kW the cost reduces of $1.95. The allowable increase is 55.5, so the increase from 500 to 520 is within the allowable range, for which the shadow price stays constant. The impact on the cost is a decrease of (520-500)*1.95 = $39. The constraint on welding is non-binding.
